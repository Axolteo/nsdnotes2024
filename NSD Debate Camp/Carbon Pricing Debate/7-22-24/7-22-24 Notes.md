# Responding to Frameworks
Respond to your opponent's framework so the judge doesn't have an existential spiral. In order to win, you have to tie the best piece of offense to the best framework. Don't justify your framework with the topic. When responding to a framework, don't use a contention turn to respond to a framework. 

Saying that carbon pricing causes more pain than pleasure as a response to a framework doesn't show how the framework is bad. Take it apart by the justifications and other parts that are important to the base of the framework. 

Don't spend amounts of time that are proportional to your opponent's time on rebuttals; spend time that's proportional to the arguments that'll help you win the round.
## Responses 
### In general
Taking your opponent's syllogisms apart from the base will negate all the rest of the impacts or justifications. Let's say you're responding to two arguments:

**syllogistic:** *a -> b -> c -> d ->* conal is goofy
**independently justified:** *z ->* conal is goofy

You want to prove that conal is not goofy. You could respond to the syllogism by denying any of the parts of the chain *(a, b, c, d)*. 

**Framework hijacks:** Also known as **framework turns.** You're gonna use your opponent's arguments to justify your own framework. Let's say that *b* is the argument that you want to hijack. Say that *b doesn't link to c* and then create a new connection that says that conal is not goofy. This makes it difficult for your opponent to leverage their meta-ethic to support their argument. This actively attacks their framework. 

**Non-interdependent syllogisms:** Where B doesn't rely on A being true. A and B make one large arrow to give the conclusion that conal is goofy. These usually make a web of justifications that the opponent has to deconstruct carefully.
### Util
Moral naturalism is the meta-ethic of utilitarianism. All knowledge, including ethical knowledge, comes from experience. The justification/warrant for that is that we can only know things in the physical world; if we cannot observe it with our senses, we cannot know about it.
### The Problem with Naturalism
Math is an example of something that contradicts naturalism. We cannot observe math with the five senses, yet we know about it and its existence through reason. This directly contradicts naturalism because it shows an example of something that we can know about without observing it.
**Naturalism's arguments are:**
1. Experience tells us knowledge. We know this from experience.
2. Experience is a reliable source of knowledge.

The problem with this is that it's basically saying "experience gives us knowledge", therefore "experience gives us knowledge". You're assuming the premise is true and then taking a conclusion that is identical to the premise. Naturalism uses naturalism to justify naturalism. 
#### This means the first problem with naturalism is that it's circular.
A naturalist might try to argue that It's apparent in morality that naturalism is the correct meta-ethic. If a naturalist doesn't rely on the first syllogism, we know that they have to rely on something besides experience to justify naturalism. If the argument that naturalism is true is based on something that is non-natural, such as the noumenal world or reason.
#### This shows the second problem: naturalism must be justified with non-naturalism. It's self-defeating.
However a naturalist attempts to justify naturalism, their speech will fall into one of those two traps. You want to number each premise and argument to help the judge keep track of the arguments.
