# Kyle lecture in general
The AFF will argue that carbon pricing solves climate change. The impact of that is climate change. The NEG will argue that the carbon price hurts the economy. Because the winner of the round is whoever has the strongest piece of offense back to the winning framework, these both impact to util meaning you want to weigh the impacts to see who has the biggest impact. The AFF will weigh climate change over econ and NEG will do the opposite. It's possible that the NEG also makes an argument that says carbon tax causes mega-climate change, which means that they both have the same impacts. You have to weigh them by the link; you have to win a link and/or weigh a link. Of course, you could make a link turn or a link defense on the link from carbon pricing to climate change or vice versa. You could also make link weighing arguments; to compare how strong your links are. There are two general categories of link weighing:
1. **Strength of link**
	1. Let's say that the AFF has a card that the AFF's plan text will reduce emissions by 50% in ten years, and the NEG has a counterplan and that counterplan has a card that will reduce emissions by 10% in ten years. You can make a strength of link weighing argument that the connection between the link and the impact is stronger than the counterplan's link is.
	2. On a K vs Fairness debate, you can make strength of link weighing arguments that the strength of link for the theory shell solving the unfairness is 100%, because if the debater loses that makes up for the unfairness, but on a K, you can weigh fairness against a K by arguing that if the judge votes for the K it doesn't solve for the impacts. 
	3. Put link weighing args on thesis extension or on the line-by-line arguments. If the round is going to hinge on that link weigh, start the speech with it and then do some extra analysis on the line-by-line.
	4. If your judge isn't good, you want to specify that if your opponent drops an argument it should default to be true. Blow up any arguments you are winning. If your link is a meta-study and the NEG's link is a study, that should help you especially if their study is included in your meta-study.
	5. Meta-studies aren't gonna make as powerful claims, so be careful with that.
