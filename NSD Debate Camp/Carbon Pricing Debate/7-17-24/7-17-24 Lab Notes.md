# Policy Debate
A type of debate where debaters act like they're passing laws. They use policy-making to implement these topics. 
## Fiat
Latin for "let it be".
We assume the piece of policy is passed. The AFF has gone to Congress and proposed the policy. In a world where the plan is passed, what would happen? Util is the most common framework, and structural violence is also really common for arguing policies. 
# Disadvantages
A core policy of debate. A disadvantage of the policy being passed. **DAs** are offense, not defense. 
### Uniqueness
A description of the **status quo**; what is happening right now. Explains why the disad matters, why it's still an issue, and why it's unique. Uniqueness cards need to be recent (why is it unique to today)

**Uniqueness:** *Max is sitting on a cliff*
### Link
How the affirmative or resolution make the issue from **uniqueness** worse. **Internal links** are a causation-type of argument (a causes b which has a certain **impact**).

**Link:** *A hotdog stand sets up below the cliff and Max jumps down to get it*
**Internal Link:** *Max rapidly plummets toward the ground*
### Impact
Explains the end state of the plan, like the terminal impact or harm.

**Impact:** *Max hits the ground and dies*
### Running a DA
Extend the **UQ-L-IM** at the very minimum for every speech. It's possible the DA could turn your opponent's case against the framework. 
#### Example
**Uniqueness:** The US currently has military presence in the Middle East. We are closer to our allies there (Saudi Arabia) and can support them.
**Link:** By pulling our military out of the Middle East, our allies' military support will be decreased.
**Internal Link:** This causes us to lose allies such as Saudi Arabia, which is an important asset to us because of their oil. They'll find new allies, like Russia and China, who will support them because of lack of US presence.
**Impact:** This will make it significantly harder for us to get oil, creating an economic recession. Countries who don't already like us (Russia and China) can convince Saudi Arabia to proliferate and cause a nuclear war.
#### Example 2
**Uniqueness:** Farid is in a debate camp to learn more about debate.
**Link:** By watching skibidi toilet during lectures, Farid won't pay attention to the lecture.
**Internal Link:** When the time comes for the debate tournament, Farid will not be good at debate and lose all of his rounds.
**Impact:** Farid will cry and his self-esteem will be demolished. 
### Responding to a DA
#### Uniqueness:
**Non-unique:** The status quo is already bad.
**Outweighs:** The current status quo is so great that the effects of the DA are negligible.

Non-uniqueness and outweighing cannot be in the same argument; one says the status quo is doing badly right now and one says it's too good to be changed.
#### Link and Internal Link:
**No link:** This argument says that the plan doesn't cause the effect the DA says it'll cause.
**Link turn:** This argument says that the plan causes the opposite of the effect. Paired with a **non-unique** argument, it turns into offense.
#### Impact:
**Impact defense:** This argument says that the NEG's impact either will not happen or is overhyped.
**Impact turn:** This argument says that the impact to the disadvantage is actually good.

You *cannot* use a **link turn** and **impact turn** in the same argument. This is known as a **double turn**, and it makes you contradict yourself. 

From the **Debate Wiki**:
> Straight turns are the most strategic response to a DA, but they’re a little tricky. A straight turn consists of reading EITHER (but not both!) a non-unique + link turn or an impact turn on a DA, and no defense. Since link turns (when paired with non-unique claims) and impact turns provide offense on the DA, the negative cannot concede out of the disadvantage and go for a different argument in the 2NR, but instead must answer the straight turn. The most strategic 2N/ARs are ones that prioritize and win one or two arguments and explain why those arguments are the most important arguments in the round. This process of choosing one or two arguments to go for is called **“collapsing”** (since one is “collapsing” the round down to the most important issues). In order to do this, since most 1NCs contain multiple DAs, the negative will concede defense in the 2NR and just focus on one DA. While this might seem counter-intuitive, it becomes incredibly strategic.
# Impact Calculus
### Magnitude
How big the impact is.
### Timeframe
The amount of time it takes to happen.
### Probability
How likely it is for the impact to happen.
### Reversibility
Whether the impact can be undone if it happens.
## Defense
- Inflation is already really high, and we haven't seen countries like Russia and China instigate with us, so the impact hasn't happened. 
- Inflation being high means that the argument that inflation rising is bad is non-unique. Additionally, even as volatility increases, the potential to make more money quickly also increases, opening up a good opportunity to bounce back from the economic recession from the COVID-19 pandemic. 
- Even if inflation increases by 0.5 percent, global warming's impact still far outweighs the impact of inflation. Climate change's impact on humanity (extinction) is much more probable than a nuclear war arising from propaganda released after economic instability. Additionally, fossil fuels account for a part inflation, meaning the switch from fossil fuels to green energy from a carbon tax will have a net good effect on community.

## Framework
Establish the framework. Assert that your contract is the only way to follow meta-ethics to prevent your opponent from using your meta-ethic as well. Also, if the framework is used by governments, you can use that to argue that your framework is something that's actually used by governments.
### 1. Meta-ethic
Establish the meta-ethic. Describe the constraints and attributes of the system. 
### 2. Reasons
Reasons why the first argument is true. Establishing that the meta-ethic is correct. Combining syllogisms and independent justifications make for a good argument. Also, internally-justifying arguments (Kant) are really good to justify your argument. 
### 3. Connect
Explain how the meta-ethic connects back to the framework. Describe how you're fulfilling the condition the meta-ethic sets. The ethic is based on the meta-ethic, meaning if you lose your meta-ethic, you're losing the framework debate.

## Contentions
Contentions are made up of syllogisms.
- **State your contention's tagline**
	- Start it with "I contend" or "contention #".
- **Read your arguments (Claim Warrant Impact)**
	- Use each of your cards as warrants.
- **Explain how your arguments tie back to your framework**
	- Show why your claim is related to the framework.

If you're arguing **NEG** and the **AFF** doesn't apply a meta-ethic, call that out and use it as a reason why your framework should be used.
