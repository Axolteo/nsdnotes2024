# Claims
## Metaphysical Claim
There is a noumenal and phenomenal world. Sensory experience cannot account for all truths and concepts, but such unaccounted concepts do exist through our understanding. I can conceive of ideas that are not instantiated in physical space, so we regard those as existing in the “noumenal world,” as independent of natural phenomena, which resides in the “phenomenal world.”
## Epistemological Claim
Claims about epistemology are claims about knowledge (what is knowledge, how do we figure out that knowledge) and truth (what is true, how do we figure out what is true). You must gather facts and truth regarding a situation to figure out what the right thing is. 
### Problem of Induction
Inductive reasoning is reasoning from example or past experience. It's generally observational. For example, the Sun has risen every morning, so I can expect that tomorrow, the Sun will rise again. You're telling yourself that "I have observed the Sun rise every morning, and I have this expectation that I can extrapolate that tomorrow, when I wake up in the morning, the Sun will rise again."
Deductive reasoning is reasoning from taking known truths and taking them to other steps. "Socrates is a man, and men are mortal, so Socrates mortal". This isn't the same as inductive reasoning, because inductive reasoning says "Past Socrates's were mortal so Socrates is mortal".
## Ontological Claim
Describes "what is" and how it matters. All agents engage in rational activity and have self-awareness.  We can understand that we are the subjects of inclinations and that we have the capacity to question them.
## Metaethical Claim
Ethics for ethics. Describes what ethics are supposed to be like, how to call an ethic good, etc. Ethics should be categorical, not contingent on empirical circumstances. Only looking to end states leads to contradictory obligations, if an action leads to a good end in one situation and a bad end in another, there's no way to determine if that action ought to be taken because it would have been both good and bad. Contingent means it depends on the situation.

## Kantian Categorical Imperative

## David Hume
Moral obligations cannot exist. There is a gap between *what is* and *what ought to be*. We cannot know *what ought to be*, only *what is*.

**Obligations:** Have to do it.
**Permissions:** Can do it.
**Prohibitions:** Cannot do it.

The best ethical theory is one that tells you what your action has to or cannot be at all times.
