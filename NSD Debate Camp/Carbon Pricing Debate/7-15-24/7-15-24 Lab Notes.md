## Introduction to LD
Of the three main styles of events in debate, LD is the only singles event, not doubles. It's called LD because it's named after the presidential debates that Lincoln and Douglass had. It's traditionally known as the values debate, since it's about the values and ethics regarding a topic. It hasn't evolved a lot in that aspect, but it's by no means the only aspect. 

In LD, there's a new topic every two months, and the topic is called the **Resolution**. (eg. Resolved: The United States ought to adopt carbon pricing). There will be two sides in each debate: AFF and NEG (affirmative and negative). At a debate tournament, you will debate each side multiple times, meaning you have to defend both sides.

If you do not respond to arguments, they are conceded. You can only respond to arguments made in the prior speech, and the only place to make new arguments is AC and NC. It's important to make sure your arguments go all the way to the end of the debate in order to not risk getting called out for dropping an argument. Mention every argument in every speech.

If an argument is not responded to, the judges consider it as true for the sake of the debate no matter how clearly bad it is.
## Speech Times
#### AFF Constructive (AC) -- 6 minutes long
AFF presents their case. It's a prewritten speech read directly off of their computer.
#### Cross-Examination -- 3 minutes long
A portion where the NEG debater asks AFF questions about their speech. Ask two types of questions:
1. **Clarification questions:** If you missed something in their constructive or you didn't understand something, you can ask what it is.
2. **Strategic questions:** If you think there is a gap in logic or an incorrect argument, you can push on that. It's for poking holes in arguments. 
A common strategy for both types of questions is to pretend your opponent wasn't being clear, not to show that you're trying to poke holes.
#### First NEG Speech/NEG Constructive (1N/NC) -- 7 minutes long
The NEG will do two things:
1. They will present their case (like the AFF Constructive) by reading off of their computer.
2. They will respond to the AFF Constructive
#### Cross-Examination -- 3 minutes long
The AFF debater will ask NEG questions about their case.
#### First AFF Rebuttal (1AR) -- 4 minutes long
The AFF will defend their own case, advance the arguments, and respond to the NEG case.
#### Second NEG Rebuttal (2NR) -- 6 minutes long
The NEG will continue to defend their own case and attack the AFF case.
#### Second AFF Rebuttal (2AR) -- 3 minutes long
The AFF will continue to defend their own case, advance the arguments, and respond to the NEG case.
## Two Parts of a Case
#### 1. Framework
LD Debate values debate. The framework will generally introduce some type of guiding ethical principle. It's very important to keep the framework and responses to the framework distinct from the topic arguments. The framework will generally not be about the topic, and it won't justify your side by itself.

A couple of examples would be maximizing happiness or promoting equality. 

**There are two strategic ways to use a framework:**
1. Justify your own arguments and why those types of impacts matter.
1. Exclude your opponent's case from the framework.
#### 2. Contention (Offense)
You will make arguments applying your framework to the topic. For example, you could argue that carbon pricing in the US would maximize happiness, or that carbon pricing harms equality. 

### There are two categories of arguments: offensive and defensive arguments.
An offensive argument is an argument that impacts back to the framework and helps a side in the framework. Make offensive arguments that connect to your own framework, but you could also make responses to your opponent's case that impact back to their framework but help your side. These are called "turns" because they turn your opponent's case against them.
A defensive argument won't necessarily link back to a framework, it will just disprove your opponent's argument. You can only win with offense, you can't just win with defense. It usually neutralizes arguments by saying it's "not bad and not good".

The way to win a debate round in LD is to figure out which debater had the strongest piece of offense to support the strongest framework.

Making turns are the best way to win novice debate.
