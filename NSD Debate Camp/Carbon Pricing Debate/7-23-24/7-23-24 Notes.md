# Responses to Kant
## Premise 1 (Metaphysical Claim)
- The noumenal world is inherently speculative
- There's no way to know what our reason is doing, it could be going to the shnoumenal world or something instead of the noumenal world
## Premise 2 (Epistemological Claim)
- We can't know that Kant is true since we don't know that we discovered a sufficient amount of a priori truths to know for sure.
- The only example provided was math (1 + 1 = 2), but this example is flawed because numbers don't have a particular existence and they are merely relational. 
- How do we know what we found is a priori truth?
## Premise 3 (Ontological Claim)
- Anything could be a reasoner, like oxygen molecules. We can't know whether it is, meaning breathing could be a violation of their freedoms and rights.
## Schmagency objection:
We can use our agency to reject our agency, which means Kant is self-defeating.
## Tailoring objection
We can tailor our universal maxims to be hyper-specific, so that ANYTHING IS ALLOWED under Kant. For example, I could say "my maxim is that I, Caroline Jia can steal from Target at 10:30 PM on a Friday". This would be universalizable because only I am the specific Caroline Jia that can do this at 10:30 and it wouldn't create a contradiction.
	Easily disprovable by showing the Categorical Imperative.

The strat against Kant debaters is to dump arguments so they'll have to take time out of rebuttals to disprove them.

**Consequences fail**
	a. they only judge actions after they occur, which fails action guidance
	b. every action has infinite stemming consequences, because every consequence can cause another consequence. Probability doesn't solve because 
		1. Probability is improvable, as it relies on inductive knowledge, but induction from past events can't lead to deduction of future events and 
		2. Probability assumes causation, we can't assume every act was actually the cause of tangible outcomes
	c. every action is infinitely divisible, only intents unify action because we intend the end point of an action -- but consequences cannot determine what step of action is moral or not. 
	d. if you're held responsible for things other than an intention ethics aren't binding because there are infinite events occurring over which you have no control, so you can never be moral as you are permitting unjust action
	e. There's no objective arbiter to evaluate consequences
	f. You can't aggregate consequences, happiness, and sadness are immutable - ten headaches don't make a migraine
You have to predict your opponent's arguments as AFF so you can load up your case against them.
