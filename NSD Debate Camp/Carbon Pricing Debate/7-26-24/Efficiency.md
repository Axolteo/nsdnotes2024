# Overview
**AFFECT** is how you present. Every moment of your speech, including cross. Every moment in a debate round is part of your presentation, and all of it can be more efficient.
1. **Value of efficiency:**
	1. Efficient speaking is correlative to effective speaking. Being an efficient speaker is not a test of how little words you need to make an argument; it's maximizing the warrants you have in your speeches. It's the ability to communicate the maximum amount of information within the limited time of your speech. Every word and moment should be directed toward helping you win. 
	2. Part of efficiency stems from two main things: bad habits and going too fast
		1. *Bad habits:* don't base your manner of speaking on debaters you may think are better than you. We are amassing a black hole of inefficiency: debaters watch other debaters who are inefficient and the cycle continues. We are becoming less and less efficient. 
		2. *Going too fast:* go slower to let your mind catch up with your words. Give yourself moments to prepare your arguments before saying them. A pause in speech sounds infinitely better than a filler word. It hides the slowness in your thought processes. 
	3. Good speaking is good writing. Good speaking is good writing. Yes, debate is a speaking activity, but it is more so a writing activity. Good speeches rely in large part on their pre-written materials and the quality of those pre-prepared materials. You all don't impromptu your AC or NC for a reason. You want to maximize the effectiveness. The problems start when debaters go onto rebuttals. You should not do rebuttals with no pre-prepped materials.
	4. For a good rebuttal, clearly and persuasively extend your arguments. Minimize the skill it takes to impromptu arguments by preparing them in the pre-round. Think: *What is my 2AR? What is my NR?* and prepare those responses in your pre-round. Efficiency isn't about speaking fast, it's about your ability to prepare and use your time well. Write out impact explanations. Explain what you want the 2AR to look like. Use blocks in the pre-round and contextualize those to your opponent's arguments. 
	5. **Example:**
		1. Record all of your rebuttals and then write out every word you wrote in that rebuttal. Transcribe the whole speech. 
		2. When you transcribe your speech into written text, it allows you to see redundancies and lets you target where you're lacking. By doing so, you develop a stronger connection between your good writing and your good speaking. You are literally writing out a better version of the words you say. 
		3. Debaters are not effective communicators. Most rebuttals, even at higher levels, are not clear and use filler words. Every second you waste could be used to make more arguments. 
	6. The meanings of words are more important than the wpm or speed at all. Your efficiency is not just a direct equation to words spent online, it's how much you can *convey* in that time. That is what efficiency is.
2. **Common inefficiencies:**
	1. In the round or debate
		1. Don't point out that things are on the flow. That just wastes time. If you say, "They did not respond to this on the flow. They cold conceded," you could just say "They dropped this".
		2. Don't overuse the word "back". "checks back", "solve back", etc. 
		3. Very long introductions. Over explanation is a massive problem. 
		4. Structuring and numbering is good. But when you say that you have three answers and then say "1, 2, 3", that is redundant. By nature of listing the arguments out, the judge already knows how many arguments there are.
		5. "things like" and "such as" are redundant. Just skip it. "Climate change causes things like extinction and war" would just be turned into "Climate change causes extinction and war". This also is a problem of confidence. It convinces the judge that you're confident about your points.
		6. "The fact that" is also useless. Filler language. It transforms your sentences into passive voice mumbo-jumbo.
		7. "probably" is also a filler word. This one is new. It makes you sound less persuasive. 
		8. "default to" or "defer to" is the most inefficient sentences. It sounds poor. It makes very little sense. Put your actual warrant there instead.
		9. "Go to the x debate", "drop down to x debate". Also applies to a recent trend. Do signpost, just don't over narrate. Visual directions for the doc are unnecessary. 
		10. "extend across". Again, this is visual direction with no use. Even the word "extend" is unnecessary, just make the argument. You are conserving more time to explain and impact out the argument.
		11. "On this question". What are you talking about? This phrasing is ineffective. It's used to conceal the scarcity of your argument; it's a cover-up for your lack of warranting. Instead, actually explain the warrant. Lines like "our evidence is surprisingly good on this question" is just saying "our card is good". 
		12. "Always going to win/never going to win". Do important judge instruction on the ballot. That is good. However, this is poor instructional framing. It doesn't access a comparison between you and your opponent's arguments. 
		13. "In a world", "at a point where", "at a point when". This is filler. It adds words but not meaning. Instead of saying that the economy won't survive in a world of nuclear war, just say that nuclear war will devastate the economy.
		14. "Right now" or "in the status quo". Poverty is not being solved right now. This phrases make something understood explicitly that was already understood implicitly. 
		15. "Actually going to be able to", "They are not actually going to be able to solve for the economy". You sound like the nerd emoji guy. "Erm, actually judge". It's also a cousin to the problems of "probably". It turns persuasive sentences to passive sentences. The easier you can draw a line between X and Y, the easier it is for the judge to understand why your arguments are better.
		16. "Which means", "The United States has a strong military, which means the hegemony is resilient." This phrase is used to connect two statements, and removes the clear forward sentence. It transforms it into a passive voice monstrosity. Just say "hegemony is resilient because the US has a strong military". X is true because of Y.
		17. "In so far as". Also passive. Arguably more monstrous than "probably" or "maybe" 
3. **Debate training**
	1. The first thing you should do is understand what makes good debate training. Here's a list.
		1. Improve faster with daily effort.
		2. Scope. Have realistic goals. Your process needs to break down small and meaningful tasks. 
		3. Do things that target multiple skills. Don't focus on just one thing. Improve your speaking sound while also working on efficiency. 
		4. Do debate at home too. Make your own separate goals. View all of this as a debate course you're making for yourself. 
	2. Make lesson plans and reflect on your progress. Too many debaters settle for "getting better" as a goal. While everything will help you get better, that doesn't mean getting better should be an end goal. Use specific and attainable goals. How you achieve it should also be specific.
	3. Research and prep and understanding your arguments.
	4. Look at debate rounds that are good at theory shells. Practice specific ideas and concepts. 
	5. One of the best debaters of all time has more bids than any other high school policy debater ever had. Whenever he was walking to class, he would whisper impact overviews to himself and he would give warrants. He would practice 2ARs and NRs. Rafael Pierri. 
	6. If something isn't a 2NR or 2AR option, don't use it. Debate is not just a speaking activity, it is a competitive activity. This should be core to your curriculum. 
	7. Aim to improve your strategies and understand its limitations and strengths. This is foundational to good debating. 

Your AFFECT is very important. You don't want to be monotonous. It is strategically bad for you to sound like a robot. Your efficiency is lost because your persuasion goes away. Maximize how much you can add to arguments with your voice. Your voice is an underutilized tool by most debaters. Good speakers are effective. Effective debaters are efficient. The things that make you unique are the tools to make your affect better.
