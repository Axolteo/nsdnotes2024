## Sweden's carbon policy helps the climate
#### Sweden as a country emits less carbon, but after the carbon policy was implemented, carbon imports went up.
> In one of the chapters of my doctoral thesis, <mark style="background: #8a5cf5; color: white; font-size: 24px; padding: 2px">I examine the carbon offshoring behavior of Swedish manufacturing firms from 2006 to 2014 using detailed data on production, trade, and emissions. </mark>For my empirical purpose, I use firm-specific instruments measuring their imports of emission-intensive goods from countries with less stringent environmental policies. Changes in these countries' export composition are then used to predict shifts in the intermediate and final goods imports of Swedish firms.
> <mark style="background: #8a5cf5; color: white; font-size: 24px; padding: 2px">The main conclusions of the study are threefold. First, in a year where the carbon content of input import of a firm is 10% higher than what it normally is, we would expect production-based CO2 emission intensity to fall by 5.3%. </mark>While <mark style="background: #8a5cf5; color: white; font-size: 24px; padding: 2px">there is a reduction of average production-based emissions, my findings indicate that this is somewhat offset by an increase in transportation emissions. </mark>Therefore, it remains uncertain what the net effect on emissions will be. My second set of results show that environmental policy arbitrage may play a role this effect. Offshoring to countries with weaker environmental regulations led to a relatively larger reduction in emissions intensities, with this effect being particularly pronounced in low-income countries. Thirdly,  I find that cleanups are larger for multi-product firms, cleaner industries  and multinational companies.
