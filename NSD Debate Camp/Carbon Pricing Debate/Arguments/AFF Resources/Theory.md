1. **Interpretation:** Debaters must have available full citations for all evidence read in round.
2. **Violation:** S/he admits that s/he does not have full citations available
3. **Standards:**
	1. **Evidence ethics:** Requiring debaters to have their citations accessible is necessary to prevent evidence distortion. Without open access to cites, there isn’t a way for the debate community to hold debaters accountable for mis-cut evidence. Without accountability, there is NO incentive to not distort evidence. Misrepresented evidence destroys the educational value of debate because academically dishonest debaters are rewarded by claiming ethos for arguments that do not actually stem from reputable sources while those who maintain academic integrity are disadvantaged. 
	2. **Effective clash:** Requiring debaters to have their citations available allows other debaters interested in the evidence presented in the round to research good responses and subsequently effectively engage in similar or identical positions. Failing to have such citations available renders debaters unable to evaluate the evidence outside of round more carefully. Thus lack of evidence citations prevents future debates from containing substantial clash of solid evidence, which links to a voter of fairness.
4. **Fairness:** Fairness is a voter because debate is a competitive activity, and if it is unfair then it loses that aspect of competitiveness. If the round was unfair, there would be no point in participating in it as a round as opposed to as a lecture. Fairness is constitutive to the notion of competition.
2. **Education:** Education is a voter because debate is an educational activity, and if it were un-educational there would be no incentive to participate in it, and schools would have no incentive to continue funding it or promoting participation.
