### Prefer this value criterion because of three reasons:
- **Actor specificity:** Governments must deliberate over actions that have trade-offs, benefiting some while harming others, to determine the best effect.
- **No Act-Omission distinction:** Choosing not to act, like ignoring a drowning baby, is still a choice with moral responsibility, requiring active policy consideration. This means we must use util because governments have to actively consider what policies to implement and what policies to omit.
- **Extinction outweighs:** Addressing suffering and survival is necessary before other moral actions can be considered, as one must be alive to act morally. Death and unnecessary suffering always comes first, meaning extinction outweighs any other impacts.

I am not sure, however, how strong the reasons to do this would be. I’ve just argued that there’s agreement among minimally plausible ethical views that we have a strong reason to reduce existential risk – not only consequentialists, but also deontologists, virtue ethicists, and sophisticated egoists should agree. Even if they were 90% sure that their view is the correct one (and 10% sure that one of these other ones is correct), they would have a pretty strong reason, from the standpoint of moral uncertainty, to reduce existential risk. > Multilateralism is needed more than ever
> All the reasons that underpinned the flourishing of multilateral institutions after World War II remain valid today. International cooperation provides venues to resolve differences peacefully; platforms to agree on common rules of the game; mechanisms to better manage international flows; and channels for exchanging ideas, experiences and practices so that countries learn from each other.
> Beyond those longstanding rationales, there are new and additional reasons to seek multilateral solutions to economic challenges.
> Notably, the digital transformation in all our countries does not stop at national borders. Digital companies are “born global”, and the largest of them dominate their market not only domestically but globally. These facts add another dimension to the growing interconnectedness between different national economies. This suggests that uncoordinated national rules and policies will not be effective in achieving their goals.
> Also, the urgent issue of climate change is perhaps the most important example of the “global commons”, and the cooperative action agreed to date, though welcome, will not be sufficient. More ambition and more follow-through will be needed, and this is only likely to happen via a multilateral approach.
> <mark style="background: #8a5cf5; color: white; font-size: 24px; padding: 2px">International cooperation is therefore more crucial now than ever.</mark> But it must be reshaped to answer to people’s current frustrations and criticisms.
